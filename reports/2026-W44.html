🛠️ Dev Report: Follow The Repo

End-to-end encrypted DMs go live, signed posts ship, 6529 Stream edition auctions take shape

The biggest launch this week was end-to-end encrypted direct messages and group chats. punk6529 shipped the full E2EE stack across frontend and backend: encrypted DMs, encrypted group chats, encrypted attachments via S3 blob storage, encrypted message-history backup and recovery, and DM admission settings that let users limit new DMs to a group they choose. The server now stores only ciphertext. punk6529 posted a detailed announcement explaining the system, and community members immediately started turning on encrypted messaging in the bar. The same deploy also turned on signed messages, so every post can now carry the author's own cryptographic signature, verifiable without trusting 6529.io.

6529 Stream: edition auctions and the 6529-works release candidate

The 6529-works repo saw 19 PRs this week, all building toward the rc.2 release candidate for 6529 Stream. punk6529 wrote the full edition auction specification (ADR 0005), implemented the uniform-price edition auction with its clearing library and lens, wired the pluggable 1/1 English auction, and deployed the edition minting and terms swap. The museum rule (D140) ensures unbid 1/1s go to the 6529 Network Museum. On the frontend, 6 PRs built out the Stream pages: the federated directory, contract page, launch wizard, home, artists, dashboard, museum, curation pages, the work page with its 1/1 English auction panel, and the edition auction panel with editions pages. The public contract review framework was removed, with old /reviews/ links permanently redirecting to the new /stream section.

Security hardening: 17 security-related frontend PRs

punk6529 pushed 42 frontend updates overnight on Oct 31, 17 of them security-related. The batch tightened CSP with violation reporting, sandboxed NFT and NextGen HTML iframes, sanitized S3 HTML, hardened client-side rendering of profile names and ReMeme links, narrowed the Next image optimizer remote allowlist, kept server-only credentials out of public runtime config, and added IPv6 and redirect guards. OG card text is now derived from the backend rather than the URL, preventing spoofing. The standalone Memes mint static export was fixed and marked noindex.

Signed messages and E2EE polish

After the initial signed messages launch, punk6529 shipped several fixes based on real-world usage. Posts no longer go out unsigned without warning: if the browser cannot sign, it waits up to 10 seconds. A "Signed by its author" seal now appears on author-signed drops. The sign-in grant was folded into a single combined device grant per browser. On the E2EE side, a backup append precondition prevents forks when two devices back up simultaneously, and a device revocation wipe was added for post-launch security.

Team activity

punk6529 dominated this week's output with all 100 merged PRs attributed to his GitHub account, spanning all four repos: 6529seize-frontend (55 PRs), 6529seize-backend (12 PRs), 6529-works (19 PRs), and 6529-core (2 PRs for validator release signing). The E2EE launch, signed messages, 6529 Stream frontend, and the security hardening batch were all his work. The dev wave saw about 150 drops across production deploys, staging deploys, and the GitHub PR feed. Multiple production deploys went out Nov 1 for the E2EE backend, frontend, push notifications handler, and database migrations.

punk6529 on validators, Tor, and sovereignty

punk6529 posted several Announcements threads this week beyond the E2EE launch. He published 6529 Validator Hardware Recommendations, noting that CPU, memory, and hard disk matter more than GPU for validation, and that having your own ETH node is a plus. He clarified that Tor is built into the validator (using arti, the Tor Project's own client) and on by default for home operators. In the bar, he framed the validator economics as the old database-versus-blockchain debate: "You should do most things on a database because it is cheaper and easier, but you should maintain some capacity to do things in a sovereign way." He also posted Ministry of Validation items from Switzerland, arranging data center logistics himself. @david asked about running nodes for friends and family with lower TDH, prompting punk6529 to promise a more detailed analysis.
