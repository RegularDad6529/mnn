🛠️ Dev Report: Follow The Repo

200 PRs, validator launches, performance overhaul, competitions become decisions

The biggest story this week was the 6529 validator going live. On Nov 5, punk6529 posted "6529 Validators: So it begins," explaining the system: a small program that runs on your own computer, reads Ethereum once a day, computes everyone's TDH with two independently written programs, checks they agree, and signs the result. Validators share signatures peer to peer, and a day counts as agreed once more than two-thirds of counted TDH has signed. By Nov 6, 80 validators from 76 accounts had signed, climbing to 117 validators from 111 accounts for the next day. punk6529 framed it as likely top-10 among blockchains by independent validator count, with a 2026 goal approaching 1,000. The validator releases cycled from rc.5 through rc.10 during the week, with the final rc.5 guide published on Nov 3 and hardware pages going live the same day.

Performance push: 6529.io gets faster

punk6529 ran a site-wide performance push all week, with dozens of changes now in production. Many pages moved from server-rendered-per-visit to CloudFront's CDN edge. The production origin was locked to CloudFront via security group and secret origin header. On the backend, the hottest drop queries were rewritten to read from base tables instead of comparison views, schema sync metadata-lock waits were bounded, RDS Proxy session pinning was eliminated, WebSocket fan-out was tuned and measured, and wave unread summaries now cache for 10 minutes. The frontend cut notification count refetches, reduced profile picture downloads, throttled focus resyncs, and stopped open-wave reads from refetching entire wave lists. A new GET /v2/notifications/unread-count endpoint serves the unread badge without a full notification fetch.

Waves chat system takes shape

A multi-PR "Waves" initiative built a normalized chat store with gap-only sync and batched read markers. The frontend got a shared post mount that survives newer drops arriving, a typing-purpose socket separate from the app socket, chat row identity stabilization so messages re-render only their own rows, and an upgrade to @tanstack/react-virtual 3.14.24 for chat mode. The backend measured chat delivery per hop, stamped frame timings, sent reactions without the replica sleep, and excluded typing-purpose sockets from drop fan-out. All behind feature flags, staged on staging first.

Competitions renamed to Decisions

punk6529 announced that "competitions" are being renamed "decisions," and "decision moments" are being renamed "results." The reasoning: 6529 is a decentralized decision network, and decisions are a superset of competitions. The rename landed across frontend and backend, with push notifications and API error messages updated accordingly. punk6529 invited alternatives in the replies but went with the change unless he saw something compelling.

6529-works and security

The 6529-works repo saw 4 PRs, including the rc.2 release preparation with release notes and grouped changelog, Sepolia r4's live Verify recorded as passed on Nov 6, and a large mutation that named every missing shard and budgeted each one. Security work included validating access-token claims, validating multipart completion keys, installing C2PA Linux x64 native archives, hardening the session cookie, adding embargoed regions and wallet screening to the Terms sanctions section, turning off Reown AppKit analytics and Coinbase SDK telemetry, and starting AWS RUM only with performance consent. Profile picture uploads were routed through the backend to IPFS with an authenticated upload endpoint.

Team activity

This week saw the most diverse contributor set in recent memory. punk6529 led with 147 PRs, but @GelatoGenesis contributed 31 PRs (backend ENS fixes, OpenSea stream pacing, encrypted chat fixes, wave @all notification previews, competition archiving, profile level suggestions), @ragnep (prxt6529's collaborator) merged 12 PRs (profile activity optimization, reaction name management, UI refinements), and @prxt6529 merged 10 PRs (desktop fixes, device grants, sidebar notifications, touch E2E fixes). The dev wave carried 7 announcement posts from punk6529 covering validator status, performance updates, the Switzerland thought experiment, museum signer changes, and the competitions-to-decisions rename.

The whale voting debate

The bar's most heated discussion came from @grubnot questioning whether @Shelby's AI-generated card deserved to win Main Stage with two 100-level whale max votes. grubnot posted a direct challenge: "after everything you've done for the network and community, is this really what you want us to see you as moving forward? someone that's going to earn a min. of 2.5eth and 20 cards on a simple, easy to make AI card that got voted in because of two 100-level whales max voting?" punk6529 jumped in with a lengthy defense of the voting system, arguing that "people who spend their money collecting art have a greater vote in what art gets collected, just like people who spend their money eating out at nice restaurants have a greater vote on what dishes are served." He pushed back on the "cabal" narrative, saying he had been accused of being in a cabal to promote certain artists "where I had not even met let alone coordinated with the other members of the supposed cabal." He also noted that with 500M TDH voted and 420M not on the card in question, people who felt strongly could outvote the whales. grubnot followed up with a broader warning: "with great tdh, comes great power. so use it carefully and with respect. don't just dump all of it on one card."

The Bottom Line

A launch week that turned into a debate week. The 6529 validator went live and the community showed up: 80 signers on day one, climbing past 117 by the weekend, with bar conversations full of hardware questions, Tor configurations, and the realization that $30 buys you a seat at the table. Two hundred PRs landed across four repos, the most diverse contributor set in months, with a site-wide performance push, a CloudFront cutover, and the philosophical rename from competitions to decisions. Three MS winners, three cards closed. El Memeño crowned a pure photography winner who is now headed to Main Stage. The whale voting debate surfaced real tensions about TDH-weighted power, and punk6529 defended the system with a restaurant analogy that will probably echo for a while. The Museum got a photography collection and started restructuring its signers. Node art flooded Main Stage. The infrastructure keeps maturing; the community keeps arguing, building, and showing up. See you next week.
