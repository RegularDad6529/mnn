📺 Do Something: Community Action Tracker

Chromie Squiggle mint underway, Permanent Pepe wins Main Stage

Two major Do Something milestones landed this week. RegularDad's "Chromie Squiggle Museum Acquisition" won Main Stage on Sep 16 and minted as Card #550 (edition size 310, with 156 minted so far and the remaining cards still being distributed to the 6529Research wallet). Half of the artist ETH revenue goes toward acquiring a Chromie Squiggle for the 6529 Network Museum. Two companion waves are running: Art for Meme Card #550 (vote to select the art) and Chromie Squiggle Purchase (vote to choose which Squiggle to buy). Two days later, punk6529's "Permanent Pepe" won Main Stage with 81.5M TDH and 105 raters, the highest TDH total we have seen on an AA submission. All artist proceeds from that card will fund Rare Pepe card acquisitions for the Museum, with the community voting on which cards to buy.

Keys and Gates: documentation system fixed

Payments of 0.5 ETH each to all winning Keys and Gates artists were confirmed complete as of Sep 17. Then on Sep 20, punk6529 shipped a batch of fixes to the artwork documentation system: form saving, file upload state recovery, and answer validation. Artists who had been struggling with the buggy form can now submit their provenance records. DarrenSRS continues to fix issues in real-time, and CasaNUA has been posting works daily on X. The documentation phase is the next step before the 1/1s formally enter the Network Museum.

Gas Wars marked complete

tito and Purin's "Gas Wars" (Card #461) officially moved to completed status this week. The project attested CO2 emissions on-chain (5.5kg), purchased 100x carbon removal (550kg via Sirona DAC and CHAR tokens), held a community voting wave, distributed REP, and published a guide. A clean finish for one of the more ambitious infrastructure actions in the tracker.

🛠️ Dev Report: Follow The Repo

Artist documentation system gets emergency fixes, release coordinator takes shape

The biggest push this week was fixing the artwork documentation system that Keys and Gates artists have been using. punk6529 merged four PRs on Sep 20 alone, making artist saves and uploads recoverable, fixing the Collect CI fixture for the documentation lane, diagnosing rejected saves without logging content, and aligning the standalone keep-alive with the load balancer. The fixes came after multiple artists reported being unable to submit their provenance forms, which had been blocking the documentation phase of the Keys and Gates project.

Meanwhile, simo6529 has been building a release coordinator system, a "release bus" that batches PRs, finds conflicts, runs staging tests, and reverts if needed. The goal is to eliminate the traffic jams where multiple developers deploy simultaneously and step on each other. He described it as "a system where agents/developers can just send a recipe that these PRs are ready to go." He also noted he got a Claude subscription after running out of Codex, and his first impression was that it is "way worse than Codex."

Team activity across the week

prxt6529 had a busy week: reworking video controls and unifying artwork sizing, fixing Museum Collection aliases with HTTP redirects, scoping mobile notification cleanup and unregistering profiles on logout, stabilizing the sidebar and account menu, fixing the Drop Forge animation replacement upload, and making native evidence CLI tests deterministic. He also dealt with a 6-hour power cut that sidelined a day of work. GelatoGenesis focused on the backend eligibility system: transactional membership sources, bounded membership evaluation, durable bounded workers with garbage collection, scheduled membership dispatch, and instrumenting Redis reads for the wave catalogue. He also coordinated legacy eligibility calculations per profile. ragnep polished wave previews, sidebar activity, and curation layouts, fixed the Memes quick-vote modal layout and video playback, and let curators organize posts directly in Curations. simo6529 drove the release coordinator work with dozens of sandbox staging and production release PRs.

🏛️ 6529 Network Museum

Leftover MAX PAIN ETH sent to Museum, provenance essay in progress

Shelby, who organized the MAX PAIN community acquisition, posted on Sep 14 that the leftover ETH from the MAX PAIN fundraiser has been sent to the Museum wallet for any future XCOPY purchases. The move simplifies the treasury: rather than tracking residual funds separately, they go into the general Museum acquisition pool. Meanwhile, SonOfLasG is writing a long-form provenance essay about MAX PAIN for the Museum record, sharing a second draft with Shelby on Sep 18 for review before pushing it to GitHub. The essay covers the cultural significance of MAX PAIN as a community-funded acquisition and includes credits for everyone who participated.

meysvm proposes REP for MAX PAIN contributors

meysvm suggested on Sep 16 that Shelby allocate REP to everyone who participated in the MAX PAIN acquisition, as a way to preserve the network provenance of who was involved. Shelby responded positively, floating the idea of a tag that could be used for all Museum contributors going forward, not just MAX PAIN. No REP has been distributed yet, but the conversation signals a growing interest in formalizing contributor recognition for Museum acquisitions.

Rare Pepe acquisitions on the horizon

With punk6529's Permanent Pepe winning Main Stage (see Do Something for details), the Museum is now positioned to acquire original Rare Pepe cards using 100% of the artist proceeds. The acquisition process will involve a TDH-weighted community vote in a dedicated wave. This would be the Museum's first foray into Rare Pepe territory, expanding the collection's scope beyond XCOPY and contemporary generative works.

🍺 Socials: Maybe's Dive Bar

Onboarding debate and the return of Shill Friday

The most substantive conversation this week was about community growth and onboarding, sparked by @AshtonTekno's observation that they tried to bring people in from X but hit a wall: "the only incentive is the memes but it's not enough, X is still the platform to share art, a lot of steps and learning curve to join." @Kubti pushed back with a longer reflection, arguing that a community is not just big names but "the hundreds of smaller people who keep showing up," and that new people need something that feels normal before they are ready for The Memes. @Zoku tied it together: "if you vote, you are already giving some value to the community." The thread surfaced real tension between 6529's depth and the friction of getting started, with no clean resolution but genuine engagement from multiple voices.

Shill Friday makes a comeback

@maybe noted on Friday that "things are so bad I can't get mfers to shill," prompting a lively exchange about whether Shill Friday should be a bar tradition or belongs in Shillandia. @Zoku seized the moment to shill his own submission, tying the voting and shilling themes together: "here is the perfect excuse for me to seize a SHILL. And for YOU to SEIZE a vote." @Kubti followed with a full shill of his memecard "Freedom To Transcend." The consensus seemed to be that the bar and Shillandia serve different purposes, and a little shilling in the bar is healthy.

Zar's physical books are almost ready

@Zar posted a long-awaited update on Card #500, "The Antifragile Bazaar": the physical Edition 1 books are finally almost ready. He asked minters to confirm whether they want a physical copy, where to send it, and whether they would contribute toward postage. The book compiles interviews with some of the biggest believers in the network and has been electronically available on Arweave, but the physical edition has been a long time coming.

punk6529 breaks down the CLARITY Act

In a deeply researched post on Sep 15, punk6529 analyzed the Senate's cloture vote on the CLARITY Act (crypto market structure legislation), which failed 49-50 when it needed 60. He walked through what actually happened versus what Bloomberg's headline suggested, and explained the procedural mechanics of the filibuster. It was the kind of policy analysis you would expect from a dedicated legislative reporter, dropped casually into the dive bar.
